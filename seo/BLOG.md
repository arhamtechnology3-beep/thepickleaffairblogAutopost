# BLOG.md — The Pickle Affair SEO Blog Automation Specification

## 0. Purpose

This file is the master instruction set for Cursor/Antigravity to manage the blog content system for:

https://thepickleaffair.com/

Primary blog:
https://thepickleaffair.com/blogs/kitchen-tales

The system currently creates and publishes 5 blog posts every day. This workflow must NOT become a low-quality mass-AI publishing system. Every article must have a distinct search intent, useful first-hand information, strong internal linking, accurate metadata, appropriate structured data, original/meaningful media where available, and a clear relationship to The Pickle Affair's products and Kathiawadi/Gujarati heritage.

The goal is to build long-term topical authority across:
- Google Search
- Google Images
- Google Discover where eligible
- Google AI Overviews / AI Mode
- Other answer engines and AI search systems
- Social discovery
- Product discovery

Do not promise rankings, traffic, indexing, or AI citations. Optimize for quality, crawlability, relevance, usefulness, and clear entity/topic relationships.

---

# 1. NON-NEGOTIABLE CONTENT RULES

## 1.1 One primary search intent = one canonical URL

Before creating any article, crawl/search the existing blog and identify:
- Existing URL
- Title
- H1
- Meta title
- Meta description
- Primary keyword/topic
- Search intent
- Published date
- Updated date
- Canonical
- Word count
- Internal links
- Products mentioned
- Category
- Similar/duplicate articles

Never create a new article when an existing URL already targets the same primary search intent unless the new article has a genuinely different intent.

If an existing article can satisfy the new intent:
- Improve/update the existing article instead of creating another URL.

If duplicate/near-duplicate pages exist:
- Select the strongest canonical page.
- Merge useful information into it.
- Redirect redundant pages when appropriate.
- Update internal links.
- Keep canonical tags correct.
- Remove or consolidate competing pages where technically appropriate.

Do not blindly delete pages.

# 3. SEO STRATEGY

## 3.1 Primary keyword strategy

Each article must have:
- 1 primary topic/keyword
- 3–8 relevant secondary terms
- Related entities and semantic concepts
- Search-intent classification

Do not force exact-match keywords unnaturally.

Example:

Primary:
- sweet mango pickle

Secondary:
- sweet mango pickle recipe
- homemade sweet mango pickle
- meethi keri achar
- meethi keri pickle
- Gujarati sweet mango pickle
- Kathiawadi sweet mango pickle
- sweet mango pickle ingredients
- how to make sweet mango pickle

Use natural language and variations. Do not keyword-stuff.

# 4. SEARCH INTENT CLASSIFICATION

Every proposed article must be classified as one or more:

- Informational
- Recipe
- Commercial investigation
- Transactional
- Comparison
- How-to

## A. SEO Metadata Layer

Required:
- SEO title
- Meta description
- Canonical URL
- URL slug
- Primary keyword
- Secondary keywords
- Search intent
- Article category
- Author
- Published date
- Updated date
- OG title
- OG description
- OG image
- Social sharing URL

Do not expose internal keyword lists to users.

---

## B. Above-the-Fold Layer

The first screen should immediately communicate:

1. What the article is about
2. Who it is for
3. Why it is useful
4. The primary answer/value

Recommended components:

- H1
- Short introductory paragraph
- Featured/hero image
- Optional "Quick Answer" box
- Author/date information
- Optional reading time
- Optional category/breadcrumb

Do not place a large promotional block before the reader gets useful information.

---

## C. Article Navigation

For medium/long articles:

- Table of Contents
- Jump links to major H2 sections
- Clear H2/H3 hierarchy

The table of contents must link to actual page anchors.

Do not create a TOC that contains headings that do not exist.

---

## D. Main Content Layer

The main article should normally contain:

### 1. Introduction
Explain the topic naturally and establish relevance.

### 2. Direct Answer
Answer the main query in 2–4 useful sentences.

### 3. Context
Explain:
- What it is
- Why it matters
- Traditional background
- Regional context
- Relevant terminology

### 4. Detailed Explanation
Cover the subject comprehensively.

### 5. Practical Guidance
Give the reader something actionable.

### 6. Original/First-Hand Information
Where available:
- Baa's method
- Family tradition
- Actual preparation
- Real product details
- Real serving practices
- Original observations

### 7. Examples
Use practical examples where they improve understanding.

### 8. Comparison
Use a table when users may confuse related products/topics.

### 9. Tips
Provide useful tips, mistakes to avoid, or best practices.

### 10. Troubleshooting
For recipes/processes, include common mistakes and solutions where useful.

# 58. REQUIRED CONTENT DEPTH

The biggest rule for the automated blog system:

DO NOT publish short, thin articles simply because the system is scheduled to publish 5 blogs per day.

Each article must have enough substance to fully satisfy its search intent.

Suggested minimum content depth:

### Simple FAQ/answer
At least 700–900 useful words when a standalone article is justified.

### Standard informational guide
Approximately 1,200–1,800 useful words.

### Comprehensive guide
Approximately 1,800–3,000+ useful words when the topic genuinely requires it.

### Pillar article
Approximately 2,500–4,000+ useful words when justified by the subject.

These are workflow guidelines, NOT Google requirements.

Never add filler.

---

# 59. MINIMUM CONTENT COMPONENT COUNT

Before publication, Cursor should check that a normal informational article contains at least:

- 1 H1
- 1 strong introduction
- 1 direct answer
- 4+ meaningful H2 sections
- Relevant H3 sections where appropriate
- At least 1 practical/useful section
- At least 1 internal-link section
- At least 1 FAQ section when questions are natural
- At least 1 relevant product/related-content section where appropriate
- Author information
- Conclusion

A long article should normally contain substantially more than this.

Recipe articles require the recipe components listed above.

---

# 60. CONTENT QUALITY DEPTH CHECK

Cursor must inspect every draft for:

### Thin-content signals
- Repeated sentences
- Generic AI language
- Same information stated multiple times
- Excessive introductions
- Keyword stuffing
- Generic conclusions
- Empty headings
- "In today's world..." style filler
- Unnecessary bullet lists
- No original information
- No practical examples
- No differentiation from competing pages

If these occur:
REWRITE before publishing.

---

# 61. REQUIRED ORIGINAL VALUE

Every article must contain at least one strong original-value category:

### Option A — First-hand expertise
Example:
Baa's traditional preparation method.

### Option B — Original process
Example:
How The Pickle Affair prepares/handles a product.

### Option C — Original comparison
Example:
Actual differences between Meethi Keri and Chhundo based on the business's products.

### Option D — Original photography/video
Actual preparation/product/serving photos.

### Option E — Proprietary business information
Only when safe and approved for publication.

### Option F — Deep practical explanation
A genuinely useful guide that fills a clear information gap.

Avoid publishing articles that could be copied unchanged onto 100 competitor websites.

Does it comprehensively answer the topic?

### Originality
Does it contain unique value?

### Expertise
Does it demonstrate genuine knowledge?

### Accuracy
Are factual claims verified?

### Structure
Is it easy to scan?

### SEO
Are title, H1, metadata and topical signals aligned?

### Internal linking
